AIR DEFENCE

How to play

You command an integrated air defence of an airbase, a command and reporting centre (CRC) and a power plant. Use time compression (keys 1 to 5, Space to pause) to skip the transit.

Shoot and scoot

Every missile a battery fires, and every minute its radar radiates, gives its position away. The bar next to each battery shows its exposure. When it fills, the enemy has located the battery: a red cross marks the spot and a counter-battery strike (loitering munitions, then strike missiles, later ballistic missiles) arrives about a minute later. Press Scoot to pack up and move 2 to 4 km, go silent, or stay and let your guns defend it. SHORAD scoots in seconds; an MR battery is out of action for about three minutes and the LR SAM for six, and missiles it has in the air need its radar. Silence lowers exposure and a move resets it. The enemy has only a few strikes a day.

Auto tempo (next to the speed buttons, on by default) runs time faster while the nearest threat is still minutes from your units: up to 16× when nothing is coming, 4× for a threat about a minute out. It slows back to your own speed as the threat closes, whenever a missile is in the air, and for a moment when one of your batteries has been located, so the engagements and decisions run at the speed you chose.

Campaign

The clock: the campaign runs on one clock. Each day the enemy flies two to four operations, and each one is a battle. A dawn probe of drones and recon comes in the early morning. One or two combined strikes come in the late morning and afternoon, when its jets have been turned round. A night strike, heavy on ballistic and cruise missiles and drones, comes late at night. Every package launches from a real enemy installation with finite stocks, and aircraft need hours on the ground between sorties. Between raids the clock stops while you plan. Intel gives the next raid's rough time and main axis, and is sometimes out by an hour or two or wrong about the axis. Advance to the next raid runs the clock straight to it. Wait 1 hour lets time pass first, so orders arrive and repairs go on before you reposition, but the enemy may come during the hour. Nothing resets between raids. Units stay where they are with the missiles they have left, damage stays until it is repaired, and the enemy remembers positions it photographed or located from your launches for most of a day, until you move the unit. Units you buy take hours to reach the depot (2 h for SHORAD and guns, 6 h for an MR battery, 10 h for an LR battery) and sit out raids until then. Before the first raid everything is instant. Repair crews restore 15 points an hour. The day's income arrives at 06:00, along with the automatic restock. Bonus objectives are set per raid. You get one offensive and one deep strike a day. After day 10 a ceasefire ends the campaign, graded Gold, Silver or Bronze by how many towns you hold.

The front: a road of towns runs from the airbase towards the enemy. Towns you hold pay income every day, let you deploy units within 25 km of them, and each one past the first cuts the enemy's raids by 10%. Once a day (from day 2), between raids, you can pay for an offensive on the next town: an assault force digs in short of it, and the enemy counter-attacks it during the next raid with drones, missiles, helicopters and jets. The assault force sets off as a column about 11 km back down the road and drives up to its start line short of the town; the left panel shows its progress and health. It halts while enemy weapons or aircraft are within 5 km or it has just been hit, so clear the air over it to keep it moving. Drones sent at it follow it, but missiles fly at the spot they were aimed at, so a moving column makes them miss. Press Escort on a fighter in the CAP panel to have it patrol just ahead of the column and take on anything hostile within 25 km of it. Keep it above 40% until the raid ends and the town is yours. Raids also go for your front town, and a town knocked flat falls back to the enemy. Taking the enemy's forward airfield at the end of the road wins the campaign at once.

Enemy SAM sites: every town the enemy holds has a SAM site with its own radar. The SAM fires at fighters within 35 km (12 km for a fighter running in low) and has a few missiles a day; the radar makes your batteries within 45 km easier to locate and calls artillery onto an assault force within 15 km. Order a fighter to Sweep a site from the CAP panel (the alert pair can do it too): it runs in low, pops up 18 km out to fire stand-off weapons, then flies home to rearm. A hit destroys the site for good; you can also pay for a deep strike on it before the battle, and taking its town overruns it.

Enemy AWACS and tanker: from day 4 an enemy AWACS flies a racetrack far back over its SAM sites, and from day 5 a tanker joins it (Last Stand: from waves 4 and 5). While the AWACS is up, enemy aircraft are warned of our shots and our missiles kill them less often; while the tanker is up, their jammers and SEAD fighters stay on station longer. Order a fighter to Hunt one from the CAP panel: it flies out after it, but the target turns for home when it sees our fighters coming, so the chase takes minutes and goes deep, into reach of the enemy's SAMs. Each one shot down is gone for good.

A smarter enemy: raids without a recon fix go for what hurts most. They look for targets few of your batteries cover, batteries that have fired most of their missiles, the assault column on the road, and, before a big missile raid, the power plant. From day 5, strike packages bring fighter escorts, and a pair of enemy fighters sweeps your CAP. Your fighters on CAP take on hostile jets that come within 35 km. SEAD pairs also send decoys ahead of them, and fire an ARM from well beyond the usual range at any radar that keeps radiating for 40 s.

Emission control presets: each radar has an Auto button that cycles through three presets. Off radiates as you order. Near radiates only while a hostile is within reach of the battery. Engage stays silent until a hostile is inside its weapons' range. A quieter radar builds less exposure and draws fewer ARMs, but it sees threats later. Recon drones shot down with photos aboard deny the enemy that intel and earn $6M each. Enemy fighters do not fly at night or in fog and storms, and their missiles are less effective in rain.

Ten days on a new random map: the mountains (and the enemy) can lie in any direction, with the sea on the far side. Before each day you read the intelligence briefing, buy units and missiles, repair damage and place your units on the map (click Move, then click the ground; LR sectors turn in 30° steps). Units must stand within 35 km of the airbase or 25 km of a town you hold, on dry, level ground. Restock all refills every launcher and tops spares up to four in one click, and can do it every morning by itself. Tomorrow's budget comes from what still works: a damaged airbase or power plant earns less. The enemy learns: attacks that hit something come back from the same direction, and radars that radiate all day draw more SEAD. Intel misses some packages, more so later on. You lose if the airbase is destroyed or you shoot down two airliners.

Training: a short guided battle that walks you through the basics step by step. Start here if you are new.

Last Stand: one long battle against waves that never stop and grow every time. Hold a wave and pick one of three reinforcements: a new unit (it arrives at a free default spot; move it where you need it), an ammunition train, engineers to patch up the damage, or money for support calls. Every wave held also pays $25M and sends 2 spare missiles to each battery. Ballistic missiles join from wave 5, so the LR SAM is always on offer until you have one. Once every package of a wave has launched you can press Next wave now to count it as held straight away for an extra $15M, with what is left of it still in the air. The enemy's counter-battery fire never lets up either: every wave brings more strikes, closer together, and from wave 3 any battery still standing where it stood when the last wave began starts the next one part-way located (30% plus 5% a wave). Keep your batteries moving between waves. Every wave launches from the enemy's own installations and draws on their stocks, which refill a little between waves: the reinforcement screen shows what each one has left, and the Deep strike card halves the fullest of them. It ends when the airbase falls or three airliners are shot down; your best score is kept.

Medals: ten career awards kept in this browser across every campaign and Last Stand run, from a clean sheet to winning on Veteran. The main menu shows which you have; hover over one to see how to earn it.

Enemy commanders: every campaign and Last Stand faces one of four, each with a style. The Beekeeper loves drones, decoys and recon; the Artilleryman sends cruise and ballistic missiles; the Wing Commander flies strike jets, SEAD and jammers, often down one axis; the Fox has no favourite but copies whatever worked much faster. The briefing names them from day 2.

Sandbox: a fixed, fully equipped defence against six scripted raids over about 45 minutes, with a panel to spawn any threat.

The picture

Every radar runs the radar range equation with Swerling-1 detection, terrain masking over a 4/3-earth horizon, ground clutter and a Doppler notch, so low cruise missiles in the valleys appear late. All reports are fused into one track table. IFF and kinematics classify and identify tracks: hostile, suspect, pending, neutral, friend.

Weapons control status

HOLD: nothing fires automatically (CIWS still defends itself). TIGHT: auto-engage hostiles only. FREE: auto-engage anything not identified friend or neutral, which includes suspects. Watch out for airliners with a failed transponder.

Weapons

LR SAM: 120 km phased array, 120° sector facing north, track-via-missile guidance, the only system that can intercept ballistic missiles (inside 40 km). It needs its own radar to hold the track. MR SAM: 32 km active radar missile that can fire on any fused track, even with its own radar silent. SHORAD: 10 km command-guided, one target at a time. CIWS: last-ditch gun, 2.5 km, best against anti-radiation and cruise missiles, with a last chance at ballistic missiles diving on it. Skyburst: 35 mm airburst gun, 4.5 km, best against drones and cruise missiles. Laser: burns down drones one at a time, never runs dry, overheats on long bursts. Gap-filler radar: a low-level radar on a tall mast that sees into the valleys. EW jammer: a jamming truck that blinds enemy aircraft sensors within 90 km (see below).

Hold fire (H, or the button on a unit): that battery or gun makes no automatic engagements, but your direct orders still fire. Useful against decoy drones, which look like attack drones but carry no warhead.

Moving units (G, or Move on a selected unit, then click the map): the unit packs up, drives and sets up again, and is out of action the whole time (radar dark, no fire). SHORAD and guns move quickly; the LR SAM takes several minutes. CAP pairs just get a new patrol station.

Upgrades (campaign planning screen): one-off purchases such as faster reload crews or hardened shelters that last the rest of the campaign.

Support calls (on a selected unit, in campaign battles): call a resupply convoy or a field repair team. Both come out of today's budget. A convoy loads at the airbase or a town you hold and drives to the battery with 4 spare missiles; the nearer depot is quicker, but trucks on the road near the front get hunted by loitering munitions, so keep them under your guns.

Campaign depth

Veterancy: crews earn kills over the campaign and rank up from Green to Trained, Veteran and Elite. Experienced crews reload faster, turn some near misses into kills and shoot guns more accurately. A replacement unit starts Green. Bonus objectives: each raid offers two (a clean sheet, no losses, kill the jammer, and so on) that pay extra money if you meet them. Recon drones: slow, high drones that photograph your units. If one gets home, the next raids aim at what it saw, so shoot them down or move the units it photographed. Strike jets (from day 4): they come in high, drop to treetop height for the last 95 km, pop up near their target and release glide bombs. Chaff and flares make them harder to hit with missiles. A fast jet with no IFF running in low inside 45 km is declared hostile automatically; further out it stays a suspect, so go FREE or mark it HOS yourself to engage it earlier. Gap-filler radars and SHORAD are the answer. Deep strikes: once a day, between raids, you can pay for a raid of our own on the enemy's drone workshops, recon drone base, forward airfield or missile launch sites. Each has a price and a chance of success; a hit cuts today's packages of that kind and the enemy sends fewer of them for two more days. Weather: each day has its own, forecast at the top of the briefing. Rain and storms shorten the reach of X-band radars (MR SAM, CIWS, fighters), cloud, fog and rain blunt the laser, a strong wind slows drones down, and in a storm no drone can fly at all. Attack helicopters (from day 3): they creep in at treetop height, hidden in the ground clutter where radars barely see them, then pop up within about 7 km of one of your units, fire two guided missiles at it and drop back down. They are only easy to see while popped up. SHORAD, guns and CIWS deal with them; units well back from the hills are safer. Quick reaction alert: once a day, if the airbase still stands, you can scramble a pair of armed fighters for $25M (the Scramble button under Combat air patrol). They take off, patrol 20 km out towards the enemy for a short sortie with two missiles each, then land for the day. Events: some mornings bring a choice, such as an arms dealer, an informant or a request from the press, each with a price.

Electronic warfare

The stand-off jammer raises the noise floor and blinds radars along its bearing; strobes from two radars are cross-fixed to locate it. SEAD aircraft fire anti-radiation missiles (ARMs) at radiating radars: go EMCON (silent) to break their lock, and turn on the decoy to draw them away. With ARM auto on, a radar shuts itself down while a hostile missile track is closing on it and comes back once it has passed (campaign units start with it on for LR, MR and early warning radars). The GNSS jammer drags GNSS-guided drones and glide bombs off their targets. Your EW jammer swamps enemy aircraft sensors within 90 km: SEAD fighters there can only find radars within 30 km, strike jets and bombers release their weapons off aim, and recon drones take no pictures. It is loud, though: SEAD fighters always hear it and will fire at it, so keep a gun next to it. Jammers and decoys stay switched on between campaign raids.

Controls

Camera: W A S D or the arrow keys pan across the map, Q and E rotate, R and F zoom, hold Shift to move faster, Home returns to the defended area. With the mouse, drag to orbit, right-drag to pan and scroll to zoom toward the cursor. Sound: the speaker button sets the volume and the alert tones, and M mutes; sound starts after your first click or key press.

Click a track in the table, on the PPI or its 3D label, then engage, assign a CAP fighter or override its identity. Click one of your units in the left panel (or its name on the map) to see its status and a list of targets it can engage. Esc, the × button, clicking the track again or a click on empty ground deselects. Scroll on the PPI to change range.

Simulation values are representative of each class of system, not of any real system.
