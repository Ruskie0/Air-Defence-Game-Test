AIR DEFENCE

How to play

You command an integrated air defence of an airbase, a command and reporting centre (CRC) and a power plant. Use time compression (keys 1 to 5, Space to pause) to skip the transit.

Campaign

Ten days on a new random map: the mountains (and the enemy) can lie in any direction, with the sea on the far side. Before each day you read the intelligence briefing, buy units and missiles, repair damage and place your units on the map (click Move, then click the ground; LR sectors turn in 30° steps). Units must stand within 35 km of the airbase, on dry, level ground. Tomorrow's budget comes from what still works: a damaged airbase or power plant earns less. The enemy learns: attacks that hit something come back from the same direction, and radars that radiate all day draw more SEAD. Intel misses some packages, more so later on. You lose if the airbase is destroyed or you shoot down two airliners.

Sandbox: a fixed, fully equipped defence against six scripted raids over about 45 minutes, with a panel to spawn any threat.

The picture

Every radar runs the radar range equation with Swerling-1 detection, terrain masking over a 4/3-earth horizon, ground clutter and a Doppler notch, so low cruise missiles in the valleys appear late. All reports are fused into one track table. IFF and kinematics classify and identify tracks: hostile, suspect, pending, neutral, friend.

Weapons control status

HOLD: nothing fires automatically (CIWS still defends itself). TIGHT: auto-engage hostiles only. FREE: auto-engage anything not identified friend or neutral, which includes suspects. Watch out for airliners with a failed transponder.

Weapons

LR SAM: 120 km phased array, 120° sector facing north, track-via-missile guidance, the only system that can intercept ballistic missiles (inside 40 km). It needs its own radar to hold the track. MR SAM: 32 km active radar missile that can fire on any fused track, even with its own radar silent. SHORAD: 10 km command-guided, one target at a time. CIWS: last-ditch guns, every round is simulated.

Electronic warfare

The stand-off jammer raises the noise floor and blinds radars along its bearing; strobes from two radars are cross-fixed to locate it. SEAD aircraft fire anti-radiation missiles (ARMs) at radiating radars: go EMCON (silent) to break their lock, and turn on the decoy to draw them away. With ARM auto on, a radar shuts itself down while a hostile missile track is closing on it and comes back once it has passed (campaign units start with it on for LR, MR and early warning radars). The GNSS jammer drags GNSS-guided drones off their targets.

Controls

Camera: W A S D or the arrow keys pan across the map, Q and E rotate, R and F zoom, hold Shift to move faster, Home returns to the defended area. With the mouse, drag to orbit, right-drag to pan and scroll to zoom toward the cursor. Sound: the speaker button sets the volume and the alert tones, and M mutes; sound starts after your first click or key press.

Click a track in the table, on the PPI or its 3D label, then engage, assign a CAP fighter or override its identity. Click one of your units in the left panel (or its name on the map) to see its status and a list of targets it can engage. Esc, the × button, clicking the track again or a click on empty ground deselects. Scroll on the PPI to change range.

Simulation values are representative of each class of system, not of any real system.
